## The journal

*Ma* is a slow journal. It publishes when an essay is finished, which is roughly once a month and sometimes not at all. There are no comments, no newsletter pop-ups and no counters. Each piece is meant to be read once, slowly, perhaps with tea.

The essays circle a handful of subjects that turn out to be one subject: walking, the seasons, small tools and the care they ask for, food that takes weeks, and the spaces between things that make the things legible.

## The writer

Mio Kurasawa repairs and binds books in a converted rice storehouse in a valley town at the end of a two-carriage branch line. She trained for six years under a binder who measured margins with her thumb, and has worked on her own since 2019: family registers, annotated anthologies, cookbooks held together by starch.

She walks to the post office the long way on Thursdays, sharpens one knife every Sunday, and rakes the gravel at the temple up the road most mornings in summer, badly.

## A note on what is real

Mio, her valley and her neighbours are invented. So is this journal: it is a design piece, a blog theme made to show how Japanese ideas of negative space can shape long-form reading on the web. The essays were written for it, and every image was painted by a small program rather than a brush. The persimmons, the swallows and the rainy season are real, and worth going outside for.

## Colophon

Headings and pull quotes are set in **Shippori Mincho**, a mincho typeface with an unusually graceful Latin. Body text is **Fraunces**, whose optical-size axis sturdies its hairlines at reading sizes. Labels use **Zen Kaku Gothic New**. All three are licensed under the SIL Open Font License and served from this site.

The text column is 34 ems wide, about 66 characters, with generous leading. On wide screens, pull quotes turn to vertical writing and hang in the right margin, the way a line of verse might be brushed down the edge of a scroll; footnotes become margin notes on the left. On a phone, both fold back into the column.
